Where UOR-R4 is today

In one paragraph. UOR-R4 is pre-alpha: 1 of 8 milestones is complete, and no model holds a useful conversation. A saved discrete geometric model passed M1's six bars on two seeds, through its integer export. That is a real language-base result. It does not establish complete replies, new kinds of answer, durable conversation, or a shippable product. This page gives the measured numbers first, then the limits and what is still open.

Correct first guesses on DEV. The saved M1 model beats both plain count tables at both seeds.

Terms are in the glossary. Claims are labelled proof, measurement or hypothesis. A passing check shows code does what it says; it is not a trained capability, and NOT_RUN never means passed.

M1 is complete

The closing artifact is antigravity-110's trunk-joint-pointer-v1-d11, a discrete 19-table uor-r4.lut-discrete-trunk/1 model exported as two seeded UORLUT01 files. It is not the separate float-trained FBS trunk. The older close grade covers bars (a)–(e); claude-211's p-f panel score adds bar (f), and M1 #2029 is closed.

The separate FBS trunk and the current failure

The float-trained FBS trunk is a second model path; it has not joined the saved discrete M1 model. Its lut-stack/5 export keeps the rotations exact and has its DEV export gap inside the bar. The current work is complete replies on new phrasings, not another replay of M1.

Claude-217 found that cycle and echo decode steps end every registered loop, but every content decision is underpowered, so greedy remains the decode. On held-out p-s:

  • Exact-reference replies are only 3, 1 and 4 of 256 at three seeds, against 38–40 on DEV.
  • Teacher-forced reply openings are 17.4%, 13.7% and 18.6%, against 80.8%, 80.2% and 79.9% on later tokens.
  • Exact turn memory keys on the turn's exact first token ids. It adds 322–337 of 1,226 openings on DEV but only 14–16 of 1,400 on p-s, so a reworded known question can find no stored key.

Claude-218 then found trunk-state memory keys that improved reply openings offline. Claude-219 then tested them in free generation on fresh p-v. Serving the point key for the first reply token where exact memory misses improves template replies by +13, +8 and +12 and exact replies by +9, +8 and +12 at three seeds. Keyed openings are right on 27–31% of cases, so this is a partial gain, not useful chat. The sign key is inconclusive at this panel size. Claude-220 then served the point key inside one float model on fresh p-w: templates improve by +41/+40/+42 at three seeds with exact claude-219 parity, while recalling TRAIN replies rather than generalizing. Claude-222 has built the integer point table, search and /8 record, with 380 library tests and a full serving audit. Claude-224 has built the panel decode and grade. Claude-227 passed retention GPU parity within the 3% bar; Claude-228 built the run's reference/read-back/BOS instruments (390 integer-library tests with one ignored and four negative gates), which held in row 0; Claude-229 found an instrument fault: the integer session uses the native writer instead of the trained whole-table nearest readout, so no bar result or throughput is reported. Claude-231 built the nearest readout and export on native and browser paths. Claude-232 then missed greedy parity in every cell: DEV first-token agreement is 291, 285 and 284 of 310 at three seeds, and p-w agreement is 911, 777 and 64 of 1,202. The mismatch must be localized before the p-x rerun.

What does not work yet

  • Useful conversation. M1's next-token bars do not establish coherent, correct complete replies.
  • New reply kinds. p-f has held-out prompt phrasings but 62.5% of its reference replies come from TRAIN. The FBS generation results make that limitation visible.
  • A joined model. The discrete M1 path and FBS trunk remain separate. Not every FBS part is served in one integer session, and accepted-model text/chat distribution remains open.
  • M2–M8. Grounded replies, durable memory, full accepted-model serving, laptop cost, reasoning/coding, distribution and alpha release are not complete.

Historical result: K16

The 19 Oct K16-A model scored 65 and 463 of 14,287, below unigram 1,226 and T2 6,300. K16-R collapsed to 19 or 20 occupied cells. These were important negative results, but they are not the current best model and must not be used to erase the later M1 close (K16 outcome).

Four older 214M Hugging Face checkpoints predate today's rules: they used softmax and matrix multiplication and are historical evidence, not the D11 product.
