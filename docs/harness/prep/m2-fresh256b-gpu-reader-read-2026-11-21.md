## The question

M2's fresh number is 0. The saved host reader passed the sealed `m2-fresh256` panel (128 of 128 pairs, notebook row of 21 Nov), but that read was trained and scored on a CPU, the owner's answer on CPU counts was "No, GPU first", and that panel is spent for this reader line. Does the reader written by the order-free TRAIN on the 4090, read by the CUDA read kernel on the 4090, carry over to values it has never seen, on a panel of the same construction drawn after the reader was fixed, by an author who is not the reader's author?

What this sheet does not claim, whatever the result:

- **The panel is not the registered `m2-fresh256`.** It is a second draw from the same instrument: the same generator, the same five strata and counts (256 rows, 128 opposite-query pairs, 16 blocks), the same grader, the same bar, new literals. Whether a count on it may stand as M2's fresh number is a decision card for the top session's decision review, with this result as its evidence; a PASS here does not by itself change `milestones.json`.
- **The wording is the published wording.** TRAIN is 20,000 synthetic cases in the panel's published statement and question forms. A pass shows the reader reads a told value from the context under that wording, for values it has not seen. It shows nothing about other wording or open conversation.
- **The panel's author is a fresh-context agent of this lab,** not another lab and not a person. It was told nothing about the reader. The lab session that wrote the reader has not read the literals; the seal is this sheet's rule and the file's place outside git, not a lock.
- **The inputs carry the two current records of a history only,** as on `m2-fresh256`: a pass on the `update` and `reassert` rows is not evidence that the reader handles an overwrite.
- **The trunk takes no part.** The reader is keyed by the question's tokens and is carried beside the M1 trunk.
- **Grading runs on the laptop's CPU.** The replies that are graded are the ids the read kernel wrote on the GPU; the grader compares them with the sealed labels. The host also finds the reply runs and pair positions for TRAIN.
- **The earlier CPU read is prior evidence.** The same reader line answered `m2-fresh256` 128 of 128 on a CPU, so a high count is expected here; this is a confirmation on a new draw through the GPU path, not a first look at transfer.

## Steps

Laptop folder `local/claude/m2-fresh-b/`; wick folder `runs/conj/gpufresh1/`.

1. Laptop. `cargo test --manifest-path docs/labs/m2-fresh-heldout-panel/instrument/Cargo.toml --locked`: all tests pass. Check the sha256 of `literals.json` and of `sealed/inputs.json` against the pins, by `shasum` only.
2. Laptop. `conjunction-probe m2-format docs/labs/m2-fresh-heldout-panel/reader-grammar.json local/claude/m2-fresh-b/sealed/inputs.json local/claude/m2-fresh-b/fmt 1 1`: writes the 256 prompts (`fmt/panel.json`) and a TRAIN of one case from the grammar's pool (`fmt/tokens.u16`, `fmt/response_mask.u8`), which is the stream of the control reader N below. Its output is kept in a file and not read beyond its exit status and the panel's sha256. Copy the three files to wick `data/m2-fresh-b/`.
3. wick. `runs/conj/gpufresh-run.sh v43 gpufresh1 PANEL_SHA256 BINARY_SHA256`. For reader A, reader B and the control reader N: the host's order-free TRAIN (`reader-save … precise,across,free2`), the GPU TRAIN (`reader-gpu-train … free2`, which exits with an error unless the GPU bytes are the host's and the same with the replies reversed), then `conjunction-probe reader-gpu OUT.uorrdr data/m2-fresh-b/panel.json 48 OUT-read.json`, whose report holds `replies`, the ids the kernel wrote for each of the 256 prompts. The script checks every pin and prints `PINS OK` or `PIN DIFFERS`; it prints counts only, no reply and no prompt.
4. Laptop. Copy the three `*-read.json` reports back. For each: `cargo run --manifest-path docs/labs/m2-fresh-heldout-panel/instrument/Cargo.toml --locked -- --literals local/claude/m2-fresh-b/literals.json --grade REPORT --reader <that reader's sha256>`, and the same again with `--swap-pairs`.
5. Record all six grade outputs whole, the sha256 of the three reports, and the script's lines, in the M2 notebook row, the decision card and #2030.

How a reply is graded: as on `m2-fresh256`. `--grade` removes the asked role's fixed opening from a reply that starts with it and calls the registered grader `grade_generated_replies_with_evaluator_oracle` unchanged: a row is complete when the reply ends on EOS and the text is exactly the accepted answer. `pairs_both_complete_distinct` counts a history's two opposite questions both complete with different answers.

## Bar (pre-registered)

- PASS: reader A and reader B each reach `pairs_both_complete_distinct` at or above 52 of 128 and complete rows at or above 104 of 256 (the bar the owner registered for `m2-fresh256` on #2030, comment 6350135678), on replies the GPU kernel wrote, where for each reader: every pin is `PINS OK`; the GPU TRAIN exits 0 with `gpu_reader_bytes_are_the_file` and `gpu_reader_bytes_are_the_same_on_a_second_run` true; `reader-gpu` exits 0 with `equal` true (the GPU read is the host read on every prompt, and every prompt is read to its end on the GPU); the report holds 256 replies; and the grade output carries that reader's sha256, the `inputs_sha256` and `labels_sha256` pinned above, and the `panel_sha256` that step 2 recorded for `fmt/panel.json`. The reported numbers are the lower pair count and the lower row count of the two readers.
- If `reader-gpu` writes its report but exits with an error (the GPU read differs from the host read, or a prompt is not read to its end on the GPU), the replies are still graded as the kernel wrote them and the counts are reported, but the result is named "bar met with a parity break" or a miss by its counts; it is not a PASS and does not go to a decision card as one. A stop is a step that writes no report, or a grade the instrument refuses.
- The question-dependence of the count is in the metric itself: a pair counts only when both opposite questions of one history are complete with different answers, so a reader that ignores the question scores 0 pairs. The four question-blind and role-swap controls of the panel (copy first, copy last, other role's value, best constant) are 0 of 128 pairs by the instrument's check.
- `--swap-pairs` is not a control of the reader. A swapped reply carries the other role's opening, which the grader does not remove, so it is 0 pairs whatever the reader does. It is run as a check that the grade depends on reply order, expected 0 of 128, and a count above 0 is a fault in the grading path that voids the grade.
- Control, a reader with almost nothing held (reader N, the same commands on a TRAIN of one case, graded under the sha256 the script records for `n.uorrdr`): must be under 13 of 128 pairs. Otherwise the count does not come from what TRAIN wrote and the result is void. This control is weak: one case holds one role's question, so N is expected at 0 by construction, and it shows only that the tables TRAIN writes are needed, not how much of TRAIN is. If N's GPU TRAIN or read stops, the control is recorded as not run; a PASS then stands on the bar and the panel's four controls, and is reported as "PASS, reader control not run".
- Otherwise the result is both readers' counts, and it is a miss for this reader configuration, not for the mechanism.
- Disclosed: the wick script prints `gpu_count` for each reader, a count of replies equal to a reference built from the prompt's own statement, before the instrument grades. It is a count only and is seen after the panel is spent. No step prints a prompt, a reply or a literal.
- A failure in step 1 is repaired without changing any source file, and the run starts again under this sheet. From the start of step 2 the panel is spent for this reader line, whatever happens next: a stop from there on gives no result for the reader concerned, which counts as 0 pairs, is recorded as an instrument stop at that step, and is not repeated on this panel. The result is fixed by the step 4 outputs. The listing of wick's `runs/conj/` after the run is recorded, to show one `gpufresh` folder.

## Minimum test power

128 pairs in 16 blocks, the construction of `m2-fresh256`, whose measured cluster-adjusted standard error at the bar is 4.32 pairs; the controls are expected at 0, so 52 pairs stands about 12 standard errors above them. Two TRAIN seeds read the same 256 prompts, so the two counts are not independent samples of the panel; they bound the spread from the TRAIN draw only. One panel draw: nothing here measures the spread across draws of literals beyond the two panels read so far.
